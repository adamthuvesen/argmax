# Arc notes are the state, not the record, and a member's chat with the person batches

The first real Arc ran four days, 57 members, about $710. Its `NOTES.md` reached 228 KB (about 57k tokens) after 176 coordinator edits, because the plan table and a running log lived in it; 48 of 57 members read it whole on their first turn, and that read rode every later turn. Most of the bill was cache reads of the same text. The coordinator prompt had said only "keep NOTES.md current".

So the arc folder now holds two files with different readers. `NOTES.md` is the current state: standing instructions, direction, what is in flight and decided, what the next coordinator needs, with an advisory ceiling of about 4,000 words that `arc_status` reports against. `LOG.md` is append-only history nobody reads unless pointed at it. Member reports stay in their own files. The alternative, a size cap Argmax enforces, was rejected: the coordinator is the only writer and the right size depends on the work, so the prompt names the cost and the tool shows the number.

The same Arc produced 93 completion notices for 57 launches. The extra 36 were replies to the person, who had opened a member's tab and talked to it; each reply woke the coordinator for a full turn on a 250k to 700k context, to write one line into the notes. Those exchanges matter to the coordinator, which recorded decisions from them, but not one at a time. A turn the person starts in a launched chat's own tab therefore no longer notifies the launcher on its own: it counts into a per-session digest that is sent after 15 quiet minutes, or folded into the next launcher-driven notice. Turns from another session, a Goal, or the scheduler are marked on the `user.message` payload and keep the immediate notice, since a wake that waits 15 minutes to report defeats itself. The digest is in memory only; a restart drops it, and `session_read` still has the exchanges.

Two smaller findings landed with it. The notice kept only the first 4,000 characters of a member's answer while the learnings section it asks for sits at the end, so 55 of 86 notices lost it; the notice now keeps that section past the cut. And the coordinator had no clock and drifted three hours in its own timestamps, so notices and `arc_status` carry the local time.
