I searched in the Argmax browser (DuckDuckGo) and read two critics' lists in full: Entertainment Weekly (July 6) and Empire (July 20). Both are "so far" lists from mid-year, so anything released after July isn't included. The search results also included "so far" lists from Rotten Tomatoes, Time Out, Variety, /Film and IMDb, but I only read their snippets.

On both lists

  • Project Hail Mary: Phil Lord and Christopher Miller's version of Andy Weir's novel. Ryan Gosling plays a science teacher on a mission to save Earth who befriends an alien called Rocky. It's a sci-fi comedy with real heart, and Empire ranks it #2.
  • Obsession: a horror film by Curry Barker, a director who came up on YouTube. A young man wishes his crush would love him more than anyone else, and it goes badly wrong. It's low-to-mid budget and became a hit through word of mouth. Inde Navarrette's performance is getting a lot of praise, and EW already calls it an early Oscar contender. Empire ranks it #3.
  • 28 Years Later: The Bone Temple: Nia DaCosta's sequel to Danny Boyle's 2025 zombie film. It's darker than the first, with standout turns from Ralph Fiennes and Jack O'Connell. Despite its January release, both lists count it among the year's best horror films.
  • The Invite: Olivia Wilde directs and stars alongside Seth Rogen, Penélope Cruz and Edward Norton. Two couples, one apartment, and an invitation to an orgy. It's a sex comedy that turns into a tender look at long-term relationships.

Empire's #1: The Odyssey Christopher Nolan's follow-up to Oppenheimer, starring Matt Damon as Odysseus and shot for IMAX. Empire calls it "gods-tier cinema," and it gets the most buzz across the search results.
